अर्जुनविषादयोग
Arjuna Viṣāda Yoga

The Yoga of Arjuna's Despair

Home  /  Bhagavad Gita  /  Chapter 1

Chapter Overview

Chapter 1 sets the dramatic stage on the sacred field of Kurukshetra. As two massive armies prepare for battle, King Dhritarashtra asks Sanjaya to describe the unfolding events. Seeing his elders, teachers, and loved ones on the opposing side, Arjuna is overcome with profound grief, moral dilemma, and attachment, ultimately laying down his weapons in despair.

Verses 1 to 47

All Shlokas

BG 1.1 Dhritarashtra's Inquiry

धृतराष्ट्र उवाच |
धर्मक्षेत्रे कुरुक्षेत्रे समवेता युयुत्सवः ।
मामकाः पाण्डवाश्चैव किमकुर्वत सञ्जय ॥ १-१ ॥

dhṛitarāṣhtra uvācha
dharma-kṣhetre kuru-kṣhetre samavetā yuyutsavaḥ
māmakāḥ pāṇḍavāśh chaiva kim akurvata sañjaya

English Translation

Dhritarashtra said: O Sanjaya, assembled on the sacred field of Kurukshetra, eager to fight, what did my sons and the sons of Pandu do?

Explanation

The King inquires about the battle through Sanjaya's divine vision, revealing his inner anxiety and attachment to his own sons over the righteous Pandavas.

BG 1.2 Sanjaya's Response

सञ्जय उवाच |
दृष्ट्वा तु पाण्डवानीकं व्यूढं दुर्योधनस्तदा ।
आचार्यमुपसङ्गम्य राजा वचनमब्रवीत् ॥ १-२ ॥

sañjaya uvācha
dṛiṣhṭvā tu pāṇḍavānīkaṁ vyūḍhaṁ duryodhanas tadā
āchāryam upasangamya rājā vachanam abravīt

English Translation

Sanjaya said: On observing the Pandava army strategically arrayed for battle, King Duryodhana approached his teacher, Dronacharya, and spoke these words.

Explanation

Duryodhana approaches Dronacharya to highlight the formidable military formation of the Pandava army, subtly attempting to bolster his teacher's resolve.

BG 1.47 Arjuna's Despair

सञ्जय उवाच |
एवमुक्त्वार्जुनः सङ्ख्ये रथोपस्थ उपाविशत् ।
विसृज्य सशरं चापं शोकसंविग्नमानसः ॥ १-४७ ॥

sañjaya uvācha
evam uktvārjunaḥ saṅkhye rathopastha upāviśhat
visṛijya sa-śharaṁ chāpaṁ śhoka-saṁvigna-mānasaḥ

English Translation

Sanjaya said: Having spoken thus on the battlefield, Arjuna cast aside his bow and arrows and sat down in his chariot, his mind overwhelmed with grief.

Explanation

Overcome by grief and moral uncertainty, Arjuna casts down his weapon, setting the stage for Lord Krishna's divine teachings in Chapter 2.
