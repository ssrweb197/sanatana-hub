Chapter Overview

Often referred to as the summary of the entire Bhagavad Gita, Chapter 2 introduces the fundamental principles of Vedic philosophy. Seeing Arjuna overwhelmed by sorrow, Lord Krishna gently rebukes his despair and surrenders his role as a mere friend to become Arjuna's spiritual Guru.

Krishna begins his teaching with Sankhya Yoga—the analytical study of the eternal Soul (Atman) versus the temporary material body (Deha). He explains that the soul is imperishable, unborn, and indestructible. Krishna then shifts to the path of selfless action (Karma Yoga) and concludes by outlining the traits of a Sthitaprajna—a person of tranquil mind and steady wisdom.

Core Wisdom

Key Teachings & Life Lessons

Timeless practical principles to master your mind and face life's challenges.

The Immortality of the Self

The physical body changes and perishes, but the inner soul is immortal. Understanding your true spiritual essence frees you from the fear of death, loss, and change.

Detachment from Results

Focus completely on your duty (Karma) without being obsessed with outcomes or rewards. Selfless effort leads to true perfection and freedom from anxiety.

Mastery Over the Senses

Uncontrolled desire leads to anger, delusion, and loss of intellect. Cultivating a calm, disciplined mind allows one to remain peaceful in pleasure and pain alike.

Key Verses

Essential Shlokas

Pivotal verses revealing the immortal soul, Nishkama Karma, and equanimity.

VERSE 13 Transmigration of Soul

देहिनोऽस्मिन्यथा देहे कौमारं यौवनं जरा ।
तथा देहान्तरप्राप्तिर्धीरस्तत्र न मुह्यति ॥ २-१३ ॥

English Translation

Just as the embodied soul continuously passes in this body from childhood to youth to old age, so also does it pass into another body at death. The wise person is not bewildered by such a change.

VERSE 20 Imperishable Soul

न जायते म्रियते वा कदाचिन्नायं भूत्वा भविता वा न भूयः ।
अजो नित्यः शाश्वतोऽयं पुराणो न हन्यते हन्यमाने शरीरे ॥ २-२० ॥

English Translation

The soul is never born, nor does it ever die; nor having once been, does it ever cease to be. It is unborn, eternal, ever-existing, and primeval. It is not slain when the body is slain.

VERSE 47 Law of Karma

कर्मण्येवाधिकारस्ते मा फलेषु कदाचन ।
मा कर्मफलहेतुर्भूर्मा ते सङ्गोऽस्त्वकर्मणि ॥ २-४७ ॥

English Translation

You have a right to perform your prescribed duties, but never to the fruits of your actions. Never consider yourself the cause of the results of your activities, nor be attached to inaction.

VERSE 63 Root Cause of Ruin

क्रोधाद्भवति सम्मोहः सम्मोहात्स्मृतिविभ्रमः ।
स्मृतिभ्रंशाद् बुद्धिनाशो बुद्धिनाशात्प्रणश्यति ॥ २-६३ ॥

English Translation

From anger arises delusion; from delusion comes loss of memory; from loss of memory comes ruin of discrimination (intellect); and from ruin of discrimination, one is completely destroyed.
